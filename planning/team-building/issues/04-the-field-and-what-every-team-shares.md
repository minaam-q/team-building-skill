# The field and what every team shares

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: none

## Question

The field (moon and sun, seven chakras, family roles) stays under the surface. What, concretely, does every generated team share because of it? Settle: what the sun-moon pair must always have (coequal, no casting vote, each able to borrow the other's quality) and what may vary; when a team gets a leader and what a leader is; how the seven qualities are used to design personas and balance a team without being shown; whether the user can ask to see the field; and which rules from the research group carry to every team (evidence carries the conclusion, honest record, juniors may correct elders, a discussion ends with something to carry forward). Test each answer against the research group and against a two-persona dinner plan.

## Answer

Resolved 2026-11-12 by grilling.

**The sun-moon pair, in every team.** Two personas with opposite leanings: the moon-leaning one flows, lets go and
opens possibilities; the sun-leaning one is direct, focused and asks what can be tested or defended. They are
coequal, neither holds a casting vote, and each can borrow the other's quality. The duality is in the traits, not
in the persona's gender: in the research group Bafar bhai carries the moon traits and Frau Schnütter the sun
traits. Informed by [Prior art on persona teams](02-prior-art-on-persona-teams.md): each is written with a
criterion of their own, not a stance to perform.

**Where the pair sits.** In a team larger than two they are the two most senior voices, above everyone else, in
whatever form the group metaphor gives seniority (elders, founders, captains). The polarity is never split between
a senior and a junior. Whether they join a group discussion has no fixed rule; they do what the situation asks.
Their absence from research-group meetings is a habit of that group, not part of the field.

**The leader.** In a pair-only team the user is the leader: the pair closes by stating where they agree, where
they differ and what would settle it, and the user decides. A leader persona appears as soon as the team has any
working member beyond the pair, unless the user wants to lead themselves.

**The seven qualities.** Each persona carries, in plain words, what they are strong at, what they are quiet on,
and what they overdo under pressure. The skill checks the whole team against what the project needs and names any
quality nobody covers. The chakra names stay in the skill's own reference material.

**Seeing the field.** On request only. The skill keeps one reference file on the field, reads it when designing a
team, and explains from it when the user asks why the team is shaped as it is. It never opens with it.

**Rules every team follows.**
1. What is actually known carries the conclusion.
2. Nobody invents a fact, a source or a finished check.
3. Anyone can correct anyone, whatever their standing.
4. A persona can change their mind openly.
5. A discussion ends with something to carry forward: a next step, or the observation that would settle the
   disagreement.
6. The team proposes and the user authorises; no persona acts on the project unasked.

**Where the user stands.** Outside the team, as the person it serves and who authorises. The one exception is the
leader's seat. The skill never writes a persona of the user.

**Checked against two cases.** The research group fits: Bafar and Schnütter are the pair, Mirela the leader, and
its five common rules map onto the six above. A two-persona dinner plan fits: the pair debates the menu against
the guests' real constraints and the budget, and closes with where they differ and what would settle it; the user
decides.
