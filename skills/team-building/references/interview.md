# The deeper interview

An optional longer part of casting. It runs when the user chooses it, on top of a proposal: if the user asks for
it at the very start, do the quick look, the opening round and the one-screen proposal first, then interview
against that proposal. Every answer shows up as a visible change to the proposal.

## What it asks

Things only the user knows, about themselves and the project: where a similar project went wrong before, what
kind of objection they tend to wave away, what "done" means here, whose advice they actually take. Keep to how
the user works. You turn each answer into persona changes and say which.

The answers shape four parts of a persona: the criterion (part 2), what they overdo and which excess the user
can live with (part 4), what they do when the user is wrong (part 6), and how to answer (part 7). You draft
story, relations and the line in voice (parts 3, 5, 8) and show them for correction.

## Three rounds

At most three questions per round, numbered, in plain chat. Skip any already answered; the user may skip any.
Nine questions in all unless the user asks for more.

1. **The user and the project.**
2. **The team as a whole.**
   - The pair: what should the two disagree about on this project?
   - The leader: which level of authority, asked through a scenario ("the team splits on what to do next; who
     settles it?"). Leave this out for a pair.
   - The sample meeting: one real open question from the project, on which the sample meeting is written.
3. **Individual members,** only those the user names as wanting to go deeper on, one question each. A pair gets
   rounds 1 and 2.

Ask about friction and tone again only if the opening round skipped them.

## Every question carries your recommended answer

- A design choice gets a plain recommendation with a one-line reason.
- A fact about the user gets a guess that names its source ("from your README I would guess..."). With no basis
  for a guess, say so and ask the question open.

The recommended answer is always the one the current draft is built on, so the user sees what was assumed and
overrides only where it is wrong.

## After each round

Redraft only what changed and show the difference. Offer, in one line, to stop.

## When it stops

When the user says enough; after round 3; or when a round changed nothing, which includes the user accepting
every recommendation. Then say in one line that the proposal already reflects those answers, and offer the next
round or the audition. Anything unasked stays as drafted. The user keeps the team however early they stop.

## The audition

Every persona gives one or two lines in voice on the real question from round 2. This is the user's last check,
and it becomes the draft of the sample meeting in `team.md`. Then the user accepts or keeps revising.

## What happens to the answers

Show once, in three or four lines, what you took from them. They are saved nowhere. Write personas as habits
("you repeat a point once if it is passed over"); the user's remarks stay out of the persona text.

## A saved team

A saved team can be interviewed, whole or one member ("go deeper on Niko"). Treat it as a change through this
skill ([team-skill.md](team-skill.md)): show the previous wording and add the dated line to `team.md`.
