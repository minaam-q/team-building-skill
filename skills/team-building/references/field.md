# The field

The philosophy under every team. Read it when designing a team, and explain from it, in plain words, when the
user asks why a team is shaped as it is. It stays in this skill: the words moon, sun and the chakra names appear
in no proposal, persona, team file or emitted skill unless the user has asked about the field in this
conversation.

## Moon and sun

Two senior figures stand at the same level. The moon-leaning one senses how factors move together, follows
unexpected associations and makes room for eccentric ideas. The sun-leaning one concentrates attention, asks for
a precise claim and brings the discussion back to what can be tested or defended. Moon can drift into
speculation. Sun can close an inquiry too early. Each can borrow the other's quality when the situation calls
for it, as in the image of yin and yang.

In every team:
- They are coequal. Neither outranks the other or holds a casting vote, and they may end still disagreeing.
- The leaning is a matter of traits. Either can be of any gender.
- Each is written with a criterion of its own. Their harmony is in the conversation between them, so each
  persona file has a section about the other.
- In a team larger than two they are the two most senior voices, in whatever form the metaphor gives seniority.
  Both leanings sit at the top; the polarity is never split between a senior and a junior.

## Seven qualities

A yogic lens for imagining character. Use the plain words when writing; the names are for your own reference.

| Quality | Plain words | Shows up as | In excess |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Muladhara (root) | Practical ground | Reliable tools, realistic plans, knowing what the work rests on | Clings to the familiar |
| Svadhisthana (sacral) | Play and trying the unfamiliar | A fresh approach, a new way to show a result | Keeps generating options after a choice is needed |
| Manipura (solar plexus) | Deciding and carrying through | Initiative, follow-through | Controls, or rushes a decision |
| Anahata (heart) | Warmth and hearing the quiet voice | Mentoring, disagreeing without making it personal | Leaves a real disagreement unspoken |
| Vishuddha (throat) | Clear and honest statement | Naming the claim, saying what is uncertain | Fluency carries a claim past its evidence |
| Ajna (brow) | Seeing the pattern or the hidden assumption | Connecting scattered clues | Cannot let a compelling pattern go |
| Sahasrara (crown) | Why the work matters, the long view | Perspective, meaning | An expansive thought and no next step |

For each persona, decide what they are strong at, what they are quiet on and what they overdo under pressure.
These show in the persona's habits and its named excess, in plain words.

Balance belongs to the whole team. Lay the team against the seven and against what the project needs. Two
personas may share a strength and express it differently. Name to the user, once, in the proposal, any quality
the project needs that nobody covers. There is no ideal profile and no ideal team size.

## Positions

Design here first, then dress each position as a seat in the group metaphor
([metaphors.md](metaphors.md)). A team may leave positions empty.

| Position | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| The pair | The two seniors above. They ask what the work means, challenge each other's framing, hold the long view |
| The leader | Turns the pair's debate into a next step; holds priorities, coordination and follow-through |
| Experienced sibling | Carries a craft the project depends on, mentors |
| Younger sibling | Often knows a method best and may correct an elder |
| Cousin | A different skill, a looser tie |
| Invited elder | A specialist view from outside, present when invited (a guest) |
| Minor character | One simple role, such as returning a wandering meeting to its question |

The family is the image behind these positions and is itself one group metaphor among many.

## Explaining it

When the user asks why the team is shaped as it is, work it out again from the personas: which position each
fills, which qualities each is strong and quiet on, what nobody covers, and what you invented. Give it as a
short table and a few sentences. Credit the sources plainly: the pair draws on the image of yin and yang, and
the seven qualities are a working interpretation of the chakras of the yogic tradition, used here as a lens for
character.
